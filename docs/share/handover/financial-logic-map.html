The one rule

Custody decides the ledger entry. The payment method only decides custody.

On a cash ride the driver is handed the whole fare, so she ends up holding our commission — she owes us. When online payment arrives we hold the fare instead, so we owe her. Same account, same screens, same reports; only the direction of the debt changes, and that is decided by one field on the trip called custody. That is why adding a payment provider later is a configuration change and not a rebuild — and why a driver who takes some rides in cash and some online still has exactly one balance.

01

A trip completes

Every finished trip produces the same three numbers, whatever the payment method: the fare is split into the platform's commission and the driver's net earnings. What differs is only who was physically handed the fare.

Build rule. Never branch on paymentMethod anywhere in financial code. Branch on custody, in exactly one place: the function that posts trip entries.

03

Someone cancels late

Both sides can cancel, and after the grace period both sides pay for it. The difference is that a driver's fee can simply be debited from an account we already hold — and a rider's cannot, because in Phase 1 she has no card. That gap is what section 04 exists to close.

Who cancelsRider ledgerDriver ledgerWaived when
Rider, after the grace period − the fee + her configured share (default 75%) Only by an admin, with a reason
Driver, after the grace period — − the driver cancellation fee She waited out the rider no-show window

Values are frozen at driver acceptance. Changing a policy mid-trip never alters a trip already running — the numbers in force were snapshotted when she accepted.

This is the leak we closed. #1764 used to credit the driver her share of the rider's fee while explicitly putting "collect it from the rider" out of scope — so SheDrive funded that share out of its own pocket, every single time. It is now in scope, via #4000.

04

The fee comes back on her next ride

A cash rider has nothing we can charge. So her unpaid fee rides along on her next trip as a surcharge, the driver collects it with the fare, and it passes through the driver's ledger back to us.

The rules of recovery

  • The surcharge is shown to her before she confirms the ride, and again on the fare summary as its own line. It is never folded silently into the fare.
  • Below the recovery threshold: oldest fee first, one per ride.
  • At or above the threshold (default 60 EGP): her whole outstanding balance comes off the next ride in one payment, and that notice cannot be dismissed.
  • A threshold of 0 turns the escalation off entirely.
  • Commission is never taken from a recovered fee. We already hold our share of it.

There is deliberately no rider booking block. The first draft of this design blocked her once her fees crossed a limit. That deadlocks: a cash rider has no card, so taking a ride is the only way she can ever clear a fee — blocking her makes the debt permanent and recovers nothing. Escalating the recovery is self-clearing. Persistent abuse is handled by an admin suspending the rider (#1740), which is a human decision, not an automatic trap. If this comes back up in review, that is the reason.

05

When she owes too much to keep working

Cash owed to SheDrive is real exposure, so there is a ceiling on it. Cross it and she cannot go online until she settles. This is the one automatic block in the whole design, and it exists because — unlike a rider — a driver has a way to clear the debt without taking another ride.

OutstandingWhat she seesCan she go online?
below 80% of the limitHer balance, nothing moreYes
80% to 100%A warning band with the amount and the limitYes
at or above the limitBlocked, with the amount owed, the limit, and a direct link to SettleNo
limit set to 0Nothing — the gate is offYes, always

A settlement releases her immediately. No batch job, no second approval, no app restart, no re-login. She hands the cash over, the admin records it, she is back online.

Handing the cash back

A settlement is a driver physically returning what she owes. Recording it posts a ledger entry — it never edits a balance.

07

Paying a driver out

Exactly the mirror image of a settlement, and it works the same way — after the fact. Finance transfers the money on its own cycle, then records the transfer, and that recording posts the debit. Recording a payout happens on the same screen as recording a settlement, because it is the same act in the opposite direction: money moved, now write it down.

StepWhat happens to the moneyLedger
Her balance goes positiveSheDrive owes her. Nothing is requested and nothing is reservedNothing posted
Finance sends the moneyOutside the system, on Finance's own cycle, to the destination on her profileNothing posted
An admin records itAmount, destination, reference, date. Impossible without a payout destination on file, and refused above her available balance− payout

A driver never requests a payout. There is no request, no approval queue, no reservation against her balance, no minimum or maximum, and no cooling-off period — there is nothing to approve, because the money has already moved before anything is recorded. Her available figure is what SheDrive owes her, not a button. She sees the payout in her statement, with its reference and date, exactly like a settlement.

Four things that must never break

Every screen, report and audit trail above assumes these. They are not implementation preferences.

#RuleWhy it matters
1 The balance is the sum of the entries. Nothing ever writes a balance. The driver's app, the admin ledger and the revenue report cannot disagree — they read the same sum.
2 Entries are immutable. No edit, no delete, and no adjustment — that action was cut on 13 September, so nothing can currently correct a mistake. It is the audit trail. An edited ledger is not evidence.
3 Posting is idempotent, keyed {event}:{trip|request}:{party}. A retried trip completion must not charge twice. This is the single most likely production bug.
4 Every entry names its cause — a trip, a receipt, a request, or an admin plus a reason. Support has to be able to answer "why do I owe this?"

Still open — these need a business decision

1. How does the cash physically come back? The screens support office cash, bank deposit, mobile wallet and field agent. Nobody has decided which of those SheDrive will actually run, or who staffs it. #1813 cannot be finalised until this is settled.

2. Payout destinations are not collected at onboarding. #4003 adds the field and makes it required before a payout is approved, but driver onboarding does not ask for a bank account or wallet number today. That needs adding to the onboarding flow.

3. #1645 — Driver sets availability status — is Removed in ADO, yet #3996 extends the go-online check it owned and #3058's completion flow depends on its availability write. No story currently owns that transition. Raise with the API team before starting #3996.

4. Deliberately deferred — worth re-confirming rather than inheriting: surge pricing, promo codes, referral credits, tips, driver bonuses, VAT and tax reporting, PDF receipts, accounting exports beyond CSV, and fee disputes. A promo mechanic in particular is the usual answer to a cold-start liquidity problem, and SheDrive is launching into exactly that.
