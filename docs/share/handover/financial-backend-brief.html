SheDrive Backend Refinement

The seven API stories and the six admin screens, and how they meet. Everything the mobile apps need, everything an operator does, and the one table both sit on.

13 stories all at New, 11 unestimated Phase 1 · cash mockups live
Story numbers are colour-coded throughout: #3991 API #1813 Admin

Start here

Nothing in this system stores a balance.

A balance is always the sum of its ledger entries, computed on read. Every story below either appends an entry or reads the sum — there is no third kind. If a story looks like it wants to update a balance field, that is the story being wrong, not the design.

01

Five things write, five things read

The whole financial core is one table with five writers and five readers. Build the table first; everything else is a consumer of it.

Design for a reversal. Do not build one yet — decided 17 Sep. Nothing in Phase 1 can correct a mis-recorded entry: the ledger is append-only, the post-adjustment action was cut, and with the waive gone that is now true of both ledgers. A settlement recorded against the wrong driver, an amount keyed as 300 instead of 30, or a fee charged to the wrong rider is permanent, and she sees it in her statement. The product owner has accepted that for now and asked that the design account for it.

What that means concretely when you build #3991: leave room for a reverse-this-entry action later — one button on an existing entry, a required reason, posting the exact opposite amount and linking the two rows. In practice, give an entry somewhere to point at another entry, and keep the idempotency key from blocking a deliberate equal-and-opposite posting. Adding that later is cheap if the shape allows it and a migration on live financial data if it does not.

The four invariants, in the order they will bite you. (3) Posting is idempotent, keyed {event}:{trip|request}:{party} — a retried trip completion charging twice is the single most likely production bug here. (1) The balance is the sum of the entries, never stored. (2) Entries are immutable — no update, no delete. (4) Every entry names its cause, because support has to answer "why do I owe this?" from the row alone.

The API surface — 7 stories

One rule, no exception, nothing to configure. writes No commission is ever taken from a recovered fee — the platform already holds its share. Why there are no API stories for the admin screens. House rule: [API] stories exist for mobile screens only; an admin screen's backend is specified inside its own [Admin] story. So when you estimate #1813 you are estimating the screen and its endpoints together. #3993 used to be the one exception — it described the same act as #4001 from the API side — and it was folded into #4001 on 17 September. There are now no exceptions.

The go-online gate already exists — answered 17 Sep. #3996 does not extend a missing story. The availability endpoint and its approval gate are owned by #1644 [API] Driver onboarding decision — go-online and offline, which is Closed (Sprint 1, 1 point) with a test suite covering pending, rejected, suspended, no-application and approved drivers. #1645 was removed as a duplicate of #1644, not because the capability was dropped. So #3996 adds one more condition — the outstanding-balance check — to a gate that already ships. Estimate it as an extension, not a new endpoint.

03

The admin screens — 6 stories

Each is one screen plus the backend behind it. Every link opens the working mockup with demo data; the list screens also honour ?state=empty, loading, error and long.

StoryScreenWhat an operator does thereEffect on the ledger
#1813 balances Sees every driver's balance, opens her ledger, records a settlement across four channels with a reference and a generated receipt, exports settlement entries as CSV. writes a settlement credit. May release her go-online gate as a side effect.
#4001 balances Records a payout Finance has already sent — amount, date, reference. Same screen as the settlement, opposite direction. writes a payout debit. This story owns the screen and the posting: idempotent on the payout reference, refused above her available balance.
#4005 rider-balances Sees riders who owe anything and opens the rider ledger. Read-only — no waive, no adjustment. The single action is escalating persistent abuse to suspension (#1740). reads. Nothing on this screen writes to the ledger. Suspension is the only block on a rider, and it is a human decision.
#3994 pricing-policies Sets the commission, both grace periods, both cancellation fees, the driver's share of a rider fee, and the driver outstanding limit with its warning band. No ledger write. Feeds #3996, #1764 and #3997. Every change audited with who, when, before and after.
#1833 reconciliation Picks one driver and a period, reads her trips, gross fares, commission, net earnings and outstanding balance, with the per-trip rows that add up to them. reads. This is the screen support opens with a driver on the phone.
#1832 reports Period revenue: trips, gross fares, commission, cancellation fees, net driver earnings, and a check panel proving gross = commission + net. reads. Shows commission earned but not how much has actually reached us. See question 1.

There is no recovery threshold — settled 17 Sep. A rider clears her entire outstanding balance on her next completed trip, every time. No escalation, no oldest-fee-first ordering, no setting on the policy screen, and one banner in the rider app rather than two. #4002 is removed because it now describes exactly what #4000 does. A rider who runs up several cancellations before her next ride is a case we deliberately defer.

There is no payout destination in the system — settled 17 Sep. Nothing is captured from a driver, nothing is stored, and nothing is verified before a payout is recorded. Getting the money to her is a manual, off-platform process for now, so the admin simply writes down the amount, the date and a reference for what Finance already did. #4003, which would have captured and required a destination, is removed. The one check that remains on the amount — refusing more than her available balance — is data integrity, not verification of where the money went.

04

Where an admin action changes an API answer

These are the couplings worth naming out loud, because they are where a change on one side silently breaks the other.

An operator does this……the ledger gets……and this API answer changes
Records a settlement (#1813) a settlement credit #3996 stops refusing her on the next go-online attempt, with no further admin action. #1781 shows a smaller outstanding.
Records a payout (#4001) a payout debit #1781 shows a smaller available, and the entry appears in her statement with its reference.
Lowers the outstanding limit (#3994) nothing #3996 applies the new limit from each driver's next go-online request. Nobody already online is knocked off.
Changes the commission or a fee (#3994) nothing #1764 and #3997 use the values snapshotted at driver acceptance, so trips already running are unaffected.
05

One thing to settle in this session — the estimate

Q1

The revenue report says we have money we have not actually received.

On a cash business, earning commission and holding it are two different events, and they can be weeks apart. A trip completes, we have earned our 20% — but the rider paid the driver, so the driver is holding our money. It only reaches us when she hands cash back and an admin records a settlement.

Today the report shows one commission number. That number is what we earned, and it reads as though it is what we have. Those are different amounts, and the gap between them is the money still out in the field. Three figures are needed, not one:

FigureWhat it meansExample month
Commission earnedWhat completed trips generated for us. What today's report already shows.20,000
Commission settledHow much of it actually reached SheDrive, through recorded settlements.14,000
OutstandingEarned minus settled — revenue we are owed that is sitting in drivers' pockets right now.6,000

That 6,000 is the number worth having. It is our credit exposure — what we lose if those drivers stop driving — and it is the only figure that says whether collection is keeping up. Revenue can look excellent while outstanding grows every month, and one number cannot show you that. It is also the same money the Driver balances screen shows per driver, totalled: one line instead of four hundred rows.

The story now spells all of this out. #1832 was rewritten on 17 September and carries 13 scenarios. The three figures are defined with their scopes, and the traps are written down rather than left to be discovered:

  • They are not an equation. Earned and settled are period figures; outstanding is a live balance covering all time. A settlement this month can clear commission earned months ago, and commission earned on the last day of the period will not be settled yet. The report must not present them as summing, or flag the difference as an error.
  • Outstanding ignores both filters. Changing the date range must not move it, and selecting a zone must not either — a driver's ledger balance is not zone-scoped. It is shown as platform-wide or hidden with a note, never shown as if it had been filtered.
  • A settlement is not always commission. Her balance can also hold a driver cancellation fee or a rider fee she collected for us, and a settlement clears the balance rather than a named part of it. Where it cannot be attributed to commission, the report states the basis it used instead of guessing quietly.
  • Recovered rider fees carry no commission and must not inflate any of the three.

What is still open: only the estimate. #1832 carries 3 points, assigned before any of this was added to it, and it now sits in Sprint 5. Re-point it in this session. The screen and the mock API still show one commission number, so the three figures need building as well as agreeing.

Out of scope — so the conversation doesn't wander

Payment-provider integration · surge and dynamic pricing · promo codes · referral credits · tips · driver bonuses · VAT and tax reporting · PDF receipts and invoices · accounting exports beyond CSV · rider or driver dispute of a fee · automated dunning · driver-initiated payout requests · a settlement day book.

In scope, and easy to skip by accident: the custody field and its branch in #3997. It ships with one value in use, and that is the point — it is the reason the ledger stays correct without assuming who collected the fare. Do not collapse the branch just because only one side of it is exercised today.
