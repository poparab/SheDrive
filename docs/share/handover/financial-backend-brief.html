SheDrive Backend Refinement

The ten API stories and the six admin screens, and how they meet. Everything the mobile apps need, everything an operator does, and the one table both sit on.

16 stories all at New, 14 unestimated Phase 1 · cash mockups live
Story numbers are colour-coded throughout: #3991 API #1813 Admin

Start here

Nothing in this system stores a balance.

A balance is always the sum of its ledger entries, computed on read. Every story below either appends an entry or reads the sum — there is no third kind. If a story looks like it wants to update a balance field, that is the story being wrong, not the design.

01

Six things write, five things read

The whole financial core is one table with six writers and five readers. Build the table first; everything else is a consumer of it.

The four invariants, in the order they will bite you. (3) Posting is idempotent, keyed {event}:{trip|request}:{party} — a retried trip completion charging twice is the single most likely production bug here. (1) The balance is the sum of the entries, never stored. (2) Entries are immutable — no update, no delete. (4) Every entry names its cause, because support has to answer "why do I owe this?" from the row alone.

02

The API surface — 10 stories

Grouped by the Feature each already hangs off in ADO. writes appends to the ledger; reads computes from it; gate refuses an action based on it.

StoryWhat it doesKindThe thing to get right
Feature 18 — Driver Earnings API
#3991 The ledger itself. Two parties, one set of rules. Entry types, immutability, idempotent posting, cause on every row. writes Build this first — the other 15 stories are consumers. Get the idempotency key right before anything posts to it.
#1781 Driver balance and statement. Returns outstanding and available as two separate numbers, plus the entries behind them. reads Never make a client interpret a sign. One of the two is always zero. Calculates nothing.
#3993 Posts the payout debit when Finance records a transfer it has already sent. writes Idempotent on the payout reference. Refused above available, refused with no destination on file. See question 1.
#4003 Captures, updates and returns the driver's payout destination. reads Onboarding does not collect this today. See question 4.
Feature 11 — Trip Completion & Cash Payment
#3997 A completed trip posts exactly one entry, chosen by the trip's custody value. writes The only place in the platform that branches on custody. Reads the fare, commission and net from settlement (#1636) — recomputes nothing.
Feature 20 — Trip Cancellation API
#1764 Late cancellation both ways: a rider's fee opens on her ledger and credits the driver her share; a driver's fee debits her unless a no-show waives it. writes Policy values are snapshotted at driver acceptance — a mid-trip policy change never alters a trip already running.
#4000 The oldest outstanding rider fee rides as a surcharge on her next completed trip, posting to both ledgers in one event. writes No commission is ever taken from a recovered fee — the platform already holds its share. Spec §3 has the worked numbers; they balance exactly.
#4002 At or above the recovery threshold, her whole outstanding balance comes off one ride instead of one fee at a time. writes Threshold of 0 disables the escalation. It never refuses a booking — that was tried and it deadlocks.
Feature 6 — Driver Home & Availability API
#3996 Refuses to put a driver online while outstanding >= limit. Returns the amount and the limit so the app can explain it. gate A settlement that clears her must release her immediately — no batch, no approval. Limit of 0 turns the gate off. See question 2.
Feature 19 — Payments — Rider API
#4004 Rider outstanding fees and statement, each fee naming the trip and date it came from. reads Read-only. She cannot pay from here — there is no way to collect from a rider between rides.

Why there are no API stories for the admin screens. House rule: [API] stories exist for mobile screens only; an admin screen's backend is specified inside its own [Admin] story. So when you estimate #1813 you are estimating the screen and its endpoints together. #3993 is the one exception in this set, and question 1 is about exactly that.

04

Where an admin action changes an API answer

These are the couplings worth naming out loud, because they are where a change on one side silently breaks the other.

An operator does this……the ledger gets……and this API answer changes
Records a settlement (#1813) a settlement credit #3996 stops refusing her on the next go-online attempt, with no further admin action. #1781 shows a smaller outstanding.
Records a payout (#4001) a payout debit #1781 shows a smaller available, and the entry appears in her statement with its reference.
Waives a rider fee (#4005) a fee_waived credit #4004 drops the fee, and #4000 no longer recovers it on her next trip.
Lowers the outstanding limit (#3994) nothing #3996 applies the new limit from each driver's next go-online request. Nobody already online is knocked off.
Lowers the recovery threshold (#3994) nothing #4002 escalates more riders to full recovery from their next booking. A booking in progress is not interrupted.
Changes the commission or a fee (#3994) nothing #1764 and #3997 use the values snapshotted at driver acceptance, so trips already running are unaffected.
05

Five things to settle in this session

Q1

#3993 and #4001 describe the same action. #3993 is written as "As the admin portal, I want to record a payout…" and #4001 as "As a super admin, I want to record a payout…". The house rule says an admin screen's backend lives inside its [Admin] story, which would make #3993 redundant. Either fold #3993 into #4001, or keep it deliberately as the pure ledger-posting concern that the driver's statement also depends on — but decide, rather than estimating both and building it twice.

Q2

#1645 is Removed in ADO, but #3996 extends it. #3996 adds a balance check to the go-online transition that #1645 owned, and #3058's completion flow depends on the same availability write. Right now no story owns that transition. Confirm where availability is actually written before #3996 is estimated — otherwise it is extending something that does not exist.

Q3

There is no way to correct a mis-recorded entry. The ledger is append-only and the post-adjustment action was cut to keep Phase 1 simple. So a settlement recorded against the wrong driver, an amount keyed as 300 instead of 30, or a payout written down twice is permanent, and she sees it in her statement. A rider fee can still be waived; the driver ledger has no equivalent. The cheapest fix is a reverse-this-entry action — one button on an existing entry, a required reason, posting the exact opposite amount and linking the two rows. Raise this before #3991 is built: retrofitting a correction path onto a live financial ledger is materially harder than shipping one.

Q4

Payout destinations are not collected at onboarding. #4003 adds the field and makes it required before a payout can be recorded, but nothing asks a driver for a bank account or wallet number today. Either onboarding grows a step, or every payout is blocked on a back-office chase the first time Finance tries to pay anyone.

Q5

The exposure figure is specified but not built. #1832's criteria call for commission earned vs settled vs outstanding, and neither the screen nor the mock API produces it. On a cash business that outstanding number — revenue earned but still in drivers' pockets — is the most important line on the report. Confirm it is in scope for #1832 and that the 3 points it carries still reflect that, since it was pointed before this scope was added.

06

Out of scope — so the conversation doesn't wander

Payment-provider integration · surge and dynamic pricing · promo codes · referral credits · tips · driver bonuses · VAT and tax reporting · PDF receipts and invoices · accounting exports beyond CSV · rider or driver dispute of a fee · automated dunning · driver-initiated payout requests · a settlement day book.

In scope, and easy to skip by accident: the custody field and its branch in #3997. It ships with one value in use, and that is the point — it is the reason the ledger stays correct without assuming who collected the fare. Do not collapse the branch just because only one side of it is exercised today.
