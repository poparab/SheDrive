How the five tracks fit together

API

7 stories AREA PATH · SheDrive

Everything that decides or records money happens here. This track owns the ledger, chooses which entry a completed trip produces, opens and recovers cancellation fees, gates a driver from going online while she owes too much, runs the payout state machine, and serves both balances back. It is also the track that enforces the four invariants — balance is the sum of the entries, entries are immutable, posting is idempotent, every entry names its cause — because nothing above it can repair a ledger that got them wrong.

Admin

6 stories AREA PATH · SheDrive

Everywhere a human has to intervene. Admin sets every number the system runs on without a code deploy, takes the cash back in and records it, records the payouts Finance has already sent, reviews a rider who owes something, and reads the one figure that says how much of SheDrive's money is currently sitting in drivers' pockets. It is also where the only block on a rider lives — an admin suspending her, deliberately a human decision and never automatic.

Driver app

4 stories AREA PATH · SheDrive\SheDrive Mobile Team

Her money, explained to her. The driver carries a signed balance — negative means she owes SheDrive, positive means SheDrive owes her — and this track is everything that shows it or clears it. She never initiates a payout; Finance sends the money and records it, and she sees it in her statement. The app never interprets the sign and never calculates a figure: the API returns what she owes and what she can draw as two separate numbers and the app renders whichever is non-zero.

Rider app

3 stories AREA PATH · SheDrive\SheDrive Mobile Team

Telling her what she owes, before she owes it again. Almost every rider's balance is zero almost always — this track exists for the ones where it is not. The whole track turns on one rule: the fee is never folded silently into the fare. It is its own line before she confirms and again on the summary, so the total she is asked for is never a surprise. She is never blocked from booking.

Before any of this can go into a sprint

1. Estimate 28 items — 13 of them are already in Sprint 5. Only #1832 and #1833 carry points, and both were pointed before this work extended them — their scope grew and the numbers did not. Three of the unestimated items were previously closed or removed (#1764, #1781, #1813) and one was rescoped (#1788): treat all four as new work, not as carry-over.

2. #1645 is Removed but still depended on. #3996 extends the go-online check that story owned, and #3058's completion flow depends on its availability write. No story currently owns that transition. Resolve it with the API team before starting #3996, or #3996 has nothing to extend.

3. Two business decisions gate acceptance criteria, not code. Nobody has decided how the cash physically comes back — which settlement channels SheDrive will actually run, or who staffs them — and #1813's criteria cannot be finalised without it.

4. There is now no way to correct a mis-recorded entry. The ledger is append-only by design, and the post-adjustment action was cut on 13 September to keep Phase 1 simple. So a settlement recorded against the wrong driver, an amount keyed as 300 instead of 30, or a payout written down twice is permanent — and she sees it in her statement. With the waive cut on 17 September this is true of both ledgers — a fee charged to the wrong rider cannot be written off either. The product owner has accepted this and asked that the design account for it; the fix, when it comes, is a reverse-this-entry action: one button on an existing entry, a required reason, posting the exact opposite amount. Decide before #3991 is built — retrofitting a correction path onto a live financial ledger is much harder than shipping one.
